<Part n="01" title="Overview">
Much of my client-facing work has been with businesses outside India — companies evaluating software, <dfn>BaaS</dfn> (banking-as-a-service: banking capabilities a company builds into its own product) and payment infrastructure. The person on the other side is usually a founder or a senior decision-maker, and the conversation crosses time zones, cultures and very different regulatory backgrounds.

My job is to turn interest into a clear, qualified opportunity: understand what they're trying to build, explain what the product actually does for them, and keep the conversation moving until there's a real decision to make.
</Part>

<Part n="02" title="The challenge">
Infrastructure is hard to buy. Before a prospect can say yes, they need to know:

- what they'd be responsible for, and what the provider handles;
- how compliance and onboarding work in their market;
- what it takes to go live, and what they'd need to provide.

A pitch that skips any of these doesn't fail on the call — it stalls weeks later. So the work is less about presenting and more about asking the right questions early.
</Part>

<Part n="03" title="What I did">
- **Ran first conversations and discovery** — their customers, current systems, licences, constraints and what they wanted to launch.
- **Explained the product in their terms** — what changes for them, what stays with the platform, and where the responsibility sits.
- **Mapped needs to an offer** — for example, a fully branded platform versus an API integration.
- **Handled commercial follow-up** — written recaps, answers to open questions and an agreed next step after each call.
- **Qualified and progressed leads** — separating real opportunities from curiosity, and moving the real ones into the pipeline.
- **Kept relationships warm** over multiple touchpoints, not a single pitch.
</Part>

<Part n="04" title="Example: positioning a white-label platform">
Closer to home, one of the products I represent in India is RBP Finivis's <dfn>white-label</dfn> forex platform — software a partner runs under its own brand. The pitch comes down to one line:

> You have the customers. We have the licence and the stack.

A partner — a fintech, a bank, or a travel, education or immigration business — can launch a fully branded platform or integrate through APIs, while RBP Finivis runs the licensed infrastructure, <abbr title="know your customer — identity verification">KYC</abbr> and compliance workflows underneath. I also produced the campaign that carries this message: [*Your brand on top. Our engine underneath.*](../ai-creative/)
</Part>
